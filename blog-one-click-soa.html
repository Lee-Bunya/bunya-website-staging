What does a "one-click Statement of Advice" really mean?

It doesn't mean the AI writes and sends your advice. It means the manual production in front of the advice disappears — and a human still approves every one.

The short answer

A one-click Statement of Advice generates a full draft from client data the firm already holds — removing the manual assembly (re-keying data into a template) that makes an SoA take half a day. It does not replace the adviser: AI drafts, a human reviews, corrects and approves before anything reaches a client. It removes the production step, never the professional judgement.

"One-click Statement of Advice" sounds like marketing hyperbole — and if it meant "the AI writes your advice and sends it," it would be. But that's not what it means, and the distinction matters both for how it works and for whether it's safe.

Why it can be one click

The Statement of Advice is the single biggest production drain in most advice practices — often half a day of work, with the paraplanner as the bottleneck. But a large share of that time isn't judgement; it's assembly: pulling client data into a document, in the firm's format, with the figures re-keyed by hand.

When the client's data already lives in one system — the fact find, the review, the fees, the file — that assembly step disappears. The document can generate itself from data that's already there, in the firm's own template and logic. That's what makes it "one click": not that the thinking is skipped, but that the manual production in front of the thinking is removed.

One click removes the assembly, not the judgement. AI drafts; a human always approves.

The human is always in the loop

This is the part that matters most, and the part responsible vendors are explicit about. A one-click SoA generates a draft. It does not send advice to a client. An adviser reviews it, corrects it, and approves it before anything reaches the client — and the system records both the draft and the approval.

That's not a limitation bolted on for caution; it's the correct design. The AI removes the production step in front of advice; it never removes the professional judgement or the accountability behind it. Advice is still the adviser's, made and owned by a person.

What it changes for the firm

  • The bottleneck moves. The paraplanner's day shifts from building documents to checking and improving them.
  • Turnaround drops. Advice that took days can be ready for review in minutes.
  • Consistency rises. Every document starts from the same firm logic, so quality doesn't depend on who built it.
  • Capacity grows. The firm can produce more advice without more production staff.

"One click" means one instruction — not unattended

Worth being precise about what "one click" means, because the honest version is more reassuring than the slogan. It doesn't mean the AI runs off and files advice by itself. It means you give one instruction — and the system runs the supervised work behind it: reading the data, applying your firm's logic, drafting the document, and stopping for your review. For a bigger job like a full onboarding, that supervision is formalised into stages with an approval gate after each one — the system does the work between gates, proposes specific changes with reasons, and waits for you. Nothing is sent, filed or changed without a person deciding.

The same applies to the fact find and the RoA

The Statement of Advice gets the headlines because it's the biggest single document — but the same one-click generation runs across the whole advice chain. The fact find assembles from data you already hold rather than being re-collected each cycle. And the Record of Advice — the high-frequency document your team produces after every review that ends in advice — generates itself too. Because it's the RoA that a busy practice churns out most, one-click RoA often saves more cumulative time than the SoA, even though each one is smaller.

All three follow the same rule: the system drafts, a human approves, nothing reaches a client without sign-off.

The honest caveat

A one-click SoA is only as good as the data feeding it. If the client record is incomplete or the fee logic is messy, the draft reflects that — which is exactly why the human review step is non-negotiable. The promise isn't "perfect advice with no effort." It's "the production is done for you; the judgement is still yours."

Key takeaways

  • "One click" removes the manual assembly of the document, not the professional judgement behind it.
  • It works because the client data already lives in one system, so nothing is re-keyed.
  • A human always reviews and approves before anything reaches a client; the system records both.
  • It's only as good as the underlying data — which is why human review is non-negotiable.
