The Xplan alternative advice firms own

Looking for an alternative to Xplan?

Xplan is the incumbent, and it does a lot. But if it feels slow, dispersed across other tools, and rented forever, there's a different path — one you own.

The short answer

The main alternatives to Xplan in Australia are other advice platforms you rent — Plutosoft, Worksorted, AdviserLogic, Midwinter and newer AI tools. Bunya is a different kind of alternative: instead of moving from one rented platform to another, it's a command centre built to your firm, in your own Microsoft 365, that you own outright — with the CRM, reviews, advice production and compliance in one system rather than a stack of tools.

If you're reading this, you've probably already concluded that Xplan isn't quite working for your firm. You're not alone — and the reasons are well documented in the Australian adviser press.

Why firms look for an Xplan alternative

Xplan holds around 60% of the market and offers one of the most comprehensive feature sets in Australia, with deep modelling and a mature template library. For some firms, that depth is exactly right. But the same firms, and many others, describe recurring frustrations:

  • It's slow and complex. Xplan's industry nickname is "the necessary evil" — a complex interface and slow innovation cycles.
  • It doesn't integrate well. Advisers report data dispersed across systems and duplicate data entry — the "Xplan plus a stack of other tools" problem.
  • Pricing is rising and opaque. Prices are widely reported to be going up, moving to usage-based, and every quote is bespoke.
  • Implementation drags. Projects can run long, and getting it shaped to your firm is real work.

The real question isn't "which platform should we rent instead?" It's "should we keep renting at all?"

Named platforms are good products in their category — Plutosoft (top-rated CRM, fast SoAs), Worksorted (liked by independents), AdviserLogic and Midwinter (modern, cloud-native). The difference here is one of model, not a claim about any single provider.

Do you have to leave Xplan all at once?

No. The transition is staged, and where a licensee still requires a specific legacy module, that can remain in place. The goal isn't a risky overnight switch — it's moving the bulk of how your firm runs into one system you own, at a pace that doesn't disrupt clients.

Is a build really cheaper than a subscription?

It's a different shape, not necessarily a bigger number. A firm serving a thousand clients already spends millions a year on cost-to-serve; against that, a one-off build is a fraction — and unlike a per-seat subscription you pay indefinitely and never own, it becomes an asset the firm holds.
