Growth

How do you grow an advice firm without hiring in lockstep?

Demand for advice isn't the constraint any more — capacity is. The firms that grow are the ones that get more from the team they already have.

The short answer

You grow without hiring in lockstep by moving production work off people and onto a system. Most of an admin or associate's day — booking reviews, creating jobs, assembling documents, reconciling revenue, pulling reports — is production, not judgement, and a platform can carry it. Do that, and the same team serves materially more client groups, so reaching the next tier no longer requires a proportional admin hire.

For as long as anyone can remember, an advice firm grew one way: by adding people. More clients meant more advisers, more associates, more admin. The firm's ceiling was set by how many people it could hire, train and hold. That model is now running into a wall.

The constraint has changed

The Australian advice profession has stopped shrinking but isn't meaningfully growing — adviser numbers have settled while demand for advice keeps climbing. There are far more Australians who need advice than there are advisers to serve them. Demand, in other words, is no longer the constraint. Capacity is.

That inverts the old growth playbook. When you can't simply hire your way to more capacity — because the people aren't there, or because every hire adds cost and management load — the firms that win are the ones that get more out of the team they already have.

Demand is no longer the constraint on an advice firm. Capacity is. That changes how you grow.

Where the hidden capacity is

Most firms are carrying more capacity than they realise — it's just locked up in production work that doesn't need a human. Consider where an admin or associate's day actually goes:

  • Booking and preparing reviews
  • Creating and routing the jobs behind each piece of work
  • Assembling advice documents and fact finds
  • Reconciling revenue and chasing what's owed
  • Pulling together reporting and compliance evidence

Every one of those is production, not judgement — and production is exactly what a system can carry. Move it off people and onto a platform, and the same team can serve materially more clients without the quality dropping or the week getting longer.

Growing without the linear hire

The practical shift is this: instead of the next 200 clients requiring the next admin hire, the system absorbs the load. The firm reaches the next tier — 1,000 groups, 1,600, beyond — on close to the same headcount, because the work that used to demand another person is now done by the platform. Hiring becomes a choice about growth ambition, not a tax on it.

This is the difference between a firm that scales and one that just gets busier. Getting busier adds people to hold the same model together. Scaling changes the model so more clients don't mean proportionally more staff.

Key takeaways

  • Adviser numbers have stalled while demand rises — capacity, not demand, is now the constraint.
  • Most firms carry hidden capacity locked up in production work that doesn't need a human.
  • Moving production onto a system lets the same team serve materially more clients.
  • Scaling changes the model so more clients don't mean proportionally more staff; getting busier doesn't.
