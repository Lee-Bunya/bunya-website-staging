The basics
What is the Command Centre, in simple terms?

It's your practice's digital front door — one operational screen that connects your CRM, email, SharePoint, Teams, phone and other data, with workflows and AI layered over the top. Instead of jumping between systems, your team works from one place.

How is it different from Power BI?

The Command Centre is the operational "my day" workspace — where your team sees their records, their tasks and takes action. Power BI is the analytical layer on top for company-wide reporting, workload and trends. They run on the same underlying data; one is for doing the work, the other for seeing the whole picture.

How is it different from a normal CRM?

A CRM stores who your clients are. The Command Centre runs what your firm does for them — reviews, jobs, advice production, revenue and compliance — with the CRM built in as the foundation. It's the system your CRM would plug into, not just another contact database.

How do all the Microsoft pieces fit together?

Think of it as a tree: the Microsoft Power Platform is the trunk, with Dataverse (the secure central data store) and the Command Centre as the operational foundation, and branches like Power BI and AI workspaces all connecting to the same controlled data.

How is Power Platform different from Dynamics?

They share the same Microsoft platform and security foundation. Power Platform lets us build a system tailored to your firm — your apps, workflows, reports and portals — rather than adopting a fixed, off-the-shelf Dynamics application.

Your team & access
Can each person see their own day when they log in?

Yes. Everyone gets a personalised view based on who they are and the work assigned to them — "Tom's day", "Charlotte's day" — so they open the system and see exactly what's theirs to do.

Can different roles have different access?

Yes. Security roles control what each person sees — a practice manager or associate can have broader access, an adviser can be scoped mainly to their own clients — down to the record and even column level. (Access always depends on being configured and tested correctly for your firm.)

Can administrators see one queue of the work assigned to them?

Yes. A task view can filter to the admin owner or queue, so the team sees all the relevant work in one place rather than hunting for it.

Can we switch between an individual view and a whole-practice view?

Yes — personal views show the signed-in user's own work, and broader practice-wide views and reports can be given to authorised roles like management.

How the work runs
Can creating a client automatically set up their SharePoint folders?

Yes — this is a demonstrated design. Creating an account triggers the agreed folder structure and links those documents straight back to the client record.

How does SharePoint connect to the client record?

Through a standard Microsoft connection: creating an account generates a configurable folder structure, and the Command Centre surfaces the files associated with that record automatically.

How are prospects kept separate from clients?

An account-type field distinguishes prospects from clients, so your views and workflows can handle them differently — a clean pipeline without mixing the two.

What role does automation play?

Power Automate is the workflow engine underneath — it moves information, triggers approvals and notifications, and connects actions across the Command Centre and your Microsoft services.

Can we track revenue and targets?

Yes. The model tracks revenue — upfront and ongoing — by financial year, and can be extended to client-count or other practice targets, feeding straight into your reporting.

AI & skills
Can repeatable AI skills make the output consistent every time?

Yes. "Skills" are reusable instructions that apply the same process and output structure each time — so a review pack, a document or an analysis comes out consistent, no matter who runs it.

Does the AI remove the need for human review?

No — and by design. AI skills reduce errors and enforce a review structure, but a person always verifies the important outputs. AI drafts; a human approves. That principle is built in.

How do you keep a human in control of the AI?

Control is built into every step, not just the end. Bigger jobs like the onboarding playbook run in stages with an approval gate after each one — the AI does the work between gates, proposes specific changes with reasons, then stops and waits. Nothing is sent, filed, renamed or changed without a person saying so; your original files are never touched (edits go to dated copies); and some compliance steps are hard-stops the system won't let anyone skip. Where information is missing, it writes "needs confirmation" rather than inventing it.

Can we ask the AI to actually do things — like create a job or update a record?

Yes. Beyond answering questions, you can ask Cowork in plain English to take actions in the Command Centre — create a job, update a client record, and the like. The safeguard is the same as everywhere else: every change is confirmed before it's saved. The AI does the work and shows you exactly what it's about to do; a person approves it. You get the speed of asking, without giving up control of what changes.

Can our team understand and adjust the AI skills themselves?

Yes. We supply the base framework, and your team can ask a skill what it does, refine its instructions, and create or share new skills over time — because it runs in your own Microsoft environment.

Can routine jobs run automatically on a schedule?

Yes — you can set up automations for the jobs that come around every week (say, a Friday recap of your work). You describe it in plain English, and it becomes a schedule you review before it goes live. The key safeguard: an automation only ever leaves a draft for you to check — it never sends on its own — and you can pause, run, rename or delete it any time.

How does the AI improve over time?

Through controlled, iterative tuning — feedback is captured, instructions and examples are refined, and the skills get better through managed updates. It's improvement you control, not an unpredictable black box.

Can you show where AI was used, for our AI governance?

Yes. The system can record when AI was used on a task, so you have an evidence trail of where and how it was involved — useful as licensee and regulatory expectations around AI transparency grow. It sits alongside the rest of your compliance evidence, keyed to the work it relates to.

Delivery, ownership & support
Is the Command Centre live anywhere already?

Yes — it's running real advice businesses today, not a prototype. (We're happy to talk through reference practices, with their permission, on a call.)

What is the difference between UAT and production?

They're the same solution in two environments: UAT holds test or sample data so changes can be verified safely, and production holds your real, live data. Changes are proven in UAT, you approve them, then they're promoted to production.

How do changes move from our feedback into the live system?

We capture your feedback, build the change in UAT, you verify it, and then it's promoted to production — a controlled process, so nothing changes in your live system without you checking it first.

How much data cleanup is needed before we start?

The best starting point is a cleaned account list, then mapping documents into the agreed folder structure. How much effort depends on duplicates, naming quality and permissions — we help you scope it honestly upfront.

Can our team change the system after handover?

Yes — it's built in your own Microsoft environment, so your team can maintain it if they develop the Power Platform capability. We also offer training and ongoing monthly support for anything you'd rather not maintain in-house.

How does our team learn to use it?

Every firm gets a tailored training library — short, captioned videos recorded on your own system, not generic help docs. They cover everything from the first steps (opening the assistant, giving it a task) through to your firm's specific skills and playbooks, and even how to build your own skills and automations. New team members can get up to speed on their own time, and the library stays as a reference.

What happens if Bunya is no longer around to support it?

The whole system lives in your own Microsoft Power Platform environment — so it keeps running, and any capable Microsoft consultant can maintain it. You're never locked to us; you own it.

If we roll it out to another business, do we start from scratch?

No. We reuse the common foundation, components and everything we've learned, then tailor the workflows, roles and data model for the next business — so each rollout is faster than the last.
