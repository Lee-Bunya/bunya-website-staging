Honest answers

The questions every principal asks.

Do we still need our CRM — or does this replace it?

The command centre has a CRM built in — client records, contacts and history sit at its core. For most firms it replaces the standalone CRM and the tools bolted around it; where it makes sense, it can run alongside one you want to keep. Either way, you're consolidating the CRM and everything around it into one system you own.

We've been burned by a platform before. How is this different?

You see it working on your own data before you commit to a build. The Blueprint is a clickable model of your practice — your dates, your jobs, your numbers. You decide to build after you've used it, not before. And you keep the Blueprint either way.

Is it really "one click" for a Statement of Advice?

Yes — because the data already lives in the system, a full SoA (or fact find) generates from a single action. It's live and in daily use at real practices. A human always reviews and approves before anything reaches a client: AI drafts, a person decides.

Isn't a custom build more expensive than software we subscribe to?

It's a different shape, not a bigger number. A 1,000-client practice already spends over $4M a year to serve clients. A one-off build is a fraction of that — and unlike a per-seat subscription you pay forever and never own, the platform is yours permanently, in your own tenancy. See what the current way is costing you →

Where does our data live, and who can see it?

Inside your own Microsoft 365 — SharePoint, Outlook, Teams. Your client data stays in your ecosystem under your governance, rather than a third party holding it. Staying inside Microsoft is exactly what unlocks the AI skills, too.

Does the AI make decisions for our advisers?

No. AI drafts, a human approves, the system records both. Every skill runs to approval gates — nothing is sent, filed or changed without a person saying so.
