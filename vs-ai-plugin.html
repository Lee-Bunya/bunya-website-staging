Own the system, or rent a layer on it

Bunya vs an AI plugin for your advice stack.

AI assistants that sit on top of your existing tools are here, and they're genuinely useful. But they solve a different problem to Bunya. One makes your rented stack smarter. The other replaces it with a system you own.

The short answer

An AI plugin for advisers (like the ones now appearing) is a smart layer on top of the tools you already rent — it reads across your CRM, custodian and planning tools and drafts things for you. Useful, but you still own nothing and still run ten systems. Bunya is the opposite model: it replaces the stack with one command centre, built to your firm, running in your own Microsoft tenancy, that you own outright — and it's built for Australian advice, not the US market.

In 2026, frontier-AI assistants for financial advisers arrived — connectors and "skills" that plug into the platforms advisers already use and automate meeting prep, notes, follow-ups and compliance checks. This is a good thing. It validates what Bunya has said all along: AI is now how advice firms will run.

But "use an AI plugin" and "run your practice on Bunya" are not the same decision. They sit at different layers, solve different problems, and leave you owning very different things.

A layer on the stack, or the stack itself

The clearest way to see it: a plugin is a better co-pilot for the cockpit you already have. Bunya is a new plane, built to your specification. Both fly — but you end up owning something completely different.

The question isn't "AI or not." It's rent a smarter layer on your existing mess, or own the system that replaces it.

AI adviser plugins are a real step forward and many firms will use them. The point here is one of model and ownership, not a criticism of any provider — and Bunya is itself built on the same frontier AI, brought into a system you own and localised for Australia.

"But isn't Bunya built on the same AI?"

Yes — and that's the point. Same frontier engine; completely different thing to own. A plugin hands you the engine as a rentable layer over your existing tools. Bunya builds that engine into an owned command centre, shaped to your firm, with your data in your tenancy, localised for Australian advice. Same power source; a different vehicle, and you own it.

"Could we just use a plugin and save money?"

You could — if what you want is your current stack, slightly smarter, still rented, still ten systems to run. If what you want is to stop running ten systems and own one that runs the whole practice, that's Bunya. Different problem being solved. Plenty of firms will use plugins; the ones who want to own their operating system come to us.

"What if a big provider builds an Australian version?"

They may, in time — though the US market is the current focus. But it would be the same model either way: a rented layer on your existing tools. Our bet, and the reason firms choose us now, is that serious Australian practices want to own their system, built to them — not rent another layer on the sprawl.
