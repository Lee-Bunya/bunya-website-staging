The difference

Bunya vs a traditional advice CRM.

A CRM stores who your clients are. It's an important tool — but it's one piece of the puzzle. Bunya is the system that runs the whole practice around it, built to your firm and owned by you. Here's the honest difference.

The short answer

A CRM stores who your clients are. Bunya is a command centre that runs what your firm does for them — reviews, advice production, revenue and compliance — with the CRM built in as the foundation. They're different categories: one is a place to store clients, the other is the system that runs the practice around them, built to your firm and owned by you.

If you're weighing Bunya against your CRM, the honest first thing to say is that it isn't a like-for-like comparison — and that's the whole point. Comparing them feature-by-feature misses what actually separates them.

To be fair to the CRMs

The established platforms do a lot well — mature templates, deep modelling, wide integrations, and years of refinement. If all you need is somewhere to store clients and produce documents, a good CRM does that, and does it well.

Bunya is for firms that have outgrown "somewhere to store clients" — the ones who want the reviews, the admin, the revenue, the compliance and the AI running as one system they own. If that's the stage you're at, the comparison stops being "which CRM" and becomes "a CRM, or the system the CRM would plug into."

The one difference that matters most

Strip everything else away and it comes down to this: every CRM is something you rent; Bunya is something you own. Rent a platform and your data lives on their infrastructure, on their roadmap, at their price, indefinitely. Bunya is built in your own Microsoft tenancy and licensed to you outright — your data, your system, your asset.

In short

  • A CRM stores clients; Bunya runs the whole practice, with the CRM built in.
  • It's a category difference, not a feature contest — don't compare line-for-line.
  • Good CRMs are good at storing and documenting; Bunya is for firms that have outgrown that.
  • The deciding line: rent a platform, or own the system it would plug into.
